Case Study: Yale School of Art Website Redesign

1. Problem Statement

The original Yale School of Art website had a visually cluttered experience that made it difficult for users to quickly understand page hierarchy, navigate content, and complete common tasks. The site lacked clear structure, consistent visual organization, and a more intuitive responsive experience for both desktop and mobile users.

My goal was to redesign the experience so it felt clearer, more modern, easier to navigate, and more accessible without losing the creative identity behind the institution.

2. Critique and Analysis

I began by reviewing the original site’s layout, typography, navigation structure, and content grouping. During this critique, I identified issues with inconsistent hierarchy, dense page composition, and limited clarity for first-time visitors trying to find important information.

I focused on the following design gaps:

  • Weak visual hierarchy that made content harder to scan
  • Navigation that felt overwhelming and less intuitive
  • Inconsistent spacing and layout rhythm across sections
  • Need for a more polished mobile-first experience

3. Redesign Process

After identifying the main usability and layout issues, I created wireframes and higher-fidelity interface concepts to reorganize the experience. I explored how to simplify navigation, improve readability, and create a stronger sense of structure across the site.

My redesign decisions focused on:

  • Stronger page hierarchy through typography and layout balance
  • Cleaner spacing to reduce visual clutter
  • More intuitive navigation patterns for desktop and mobile
  • Responsive design decisions that preserved usability across devices
  • A more modern UI while still keeping the project visually distinctive

4. Testing and Feedback

I evaluated the redesign through critique, feedback, and review of the user flow. I looked at whether the new design improved navigation clarity, readability, and the overall ease of moving through the site.

Based on feedback, I refined several parts of the design:

  • Simplified navigation labels to make sections easier to understand
  • Adjusted spacing and grouping to improve readability
  • Refined visual hierarchy so key content stood out more clearly
  • Improved mobile responsiveness and section flow

These updates helped create a more organized and user-friendly final experience compared to the original version.

5. Final Product

The final redesign presents a more accessible, structured, and modern version of the Yale School of Art website. Compared to the original design, the updated experience offers clearer navigation, improved layout consistency, and a stronger responsive interface for users on multiple screen sizes.
